Quality management in manufacturing and supply chain processes is essential to ensure that products meet required standards and regulations. Below are explanations of key quality management subprocesses along with examples:

1. Enable Quality Management
Definition:
This subprocess involves setting up the quality management framework in a system or organization. It includes enabling features, defining quality inspection rules, test parameters, and assigning responsibilities for quality checks.

Key Activities:

Define quality inspection plans

Set inspection types (e.g., inbound, production, outbound)

Configure test instruments and quality criteria

Assign roles and responsibilities

Example:
A pharmaceutical company implements SAP Quality Management (QM) and enables inspection at the goods receipt stage. When raw materials arrive from a supplier, the system automatically triggers a quality inspection lot based on the defined inspection type and plan.

2. Nonconformance Management
Definition:
This subprocess handles situations where a product, material, or process does not meet predefined quality standards. It involves documenting, analyzing, and resolving quality issues.

Key Activities:

Record nonconformance (defect or deviation)

Classify and categorize the issue

Perform root cause analysis

Define corrective and preventive actions (CAPA)

Track closure and effectiveness

Example:
A batch of automotive brake pads fails the hardness test during inspection. A nonconformance record is created, identifying the issue as “excessive softness.” The root cause is found to be incorrect oven temperature. The corrective action involves recalibrating the oven, and preventive training is given to operators.

3. Quarantine Order
Definition:
A quarantine order is used to temporarily isolate goods that require further inspection or have an unresolved quality issue. This ensures that such goods are not used or shipped until the issue is resolved.

Key Activities:

Isolate goods in a separate storage area

Trigger inspection or analysis

Block usage or movement until release

Link to inspection lot or nonconformance

Example:
During inbound inspection at a food processing company, a shipment of rice has an unusual odor. The entire lot is moved to a "quarantine" storage bin. A quarantine order is created, and lab testing is initiated. Only after test results confirm the product's safety is the quarantine order lifted and goods released to inventory.

4. Inventory Blocking
Definition:
Inventory blocking prevents specific materials or stock from being used in production, sales, or transfers due to quality concerns or administrative holds.

Key Activities:

Set material or batch status as blocked

Prevent goods movement

Release only after inspection, approval, or manual intervention

Example:
A cosmetics manufacturer finds microbial contamination in one batch of face cream. This batch is blocked in the inventory system. Any attempt to use this batch in production or shipping will fail. The batch remains blocked until rework or disposal is approved.
